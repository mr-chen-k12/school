01 · Why we practice

Learning mathematics

The goal is not to get every answer immediately. The goal is to build understanding by thinking, trying, discussing, revising, and trying again.

“Struggling with a problem is not failing—it is part of learning mathematics.”

Students are expected to make a genuine attempt before checking an answer. Afterward, they should compare, ask questions, and use the answer or feedback to understand what worked and what needs to change.

02 · Our daily routine

How class usually works

A predictable routine helps students begin independently, learn from a partner, and then connect their ideas during a whole-class conversation.

1 Enter & begin

DeltaMath warm-up

Students begin DeltaMath with paper and pencil visible and ready to show their work.

2 Prepare

Notebook or IM workbook

After the warm-up, students open their notebook or retrieve the IM Math workbook from its classroom location.

3 You

Think independently

Each student first reads, attempts, and records the problem independently.

4 You All

Discuss with an elbow partner

Partners compare strategies, explain their reasoning, ask questions, and make revisions.

5 We

Build understanding together

The whole class discusses the problem while Mr. Chen writes live on an iPad and displays the thinking on the classroom TV.

6 Record & reflect

Write, question, and revise

Students record the displayed mathematics in their workbook, ask questions, and revise their work as understanding grows.

03 · Student expectations

What successful participation looks like

Students should expect to be challenged. They are not expected to understand everything on the first try, but they are expected to participate in the process.

Students will

  • Arrive on time with materials ready.
  • Attempt problems before checking an answer.
  • Write mathematical work and explain their reasoning.
  • Discuss ideas respectfully with an elbow partner and the class.
  • Ask questions and communicate when they do not understand yet.
  • Use feedback and additional practice to improve.

Bring every day

  • A dedicated mathematics notebook
  • Pencils
  • Paper for showing work
  • A handheld sharpener and eraser when using wooden pencils

These materials should be visible and ready at the beginning of class.

05 · Grading

Practice matters most

The grade is weighted to emphasize the daily work through which students develop mathematical understanding and learning habits.

20%
80%

20% assessments · 80% classwork and homework

Assessments include tests and other opportunities to demonstrate learning. Classwork and homework include daily mathematical practice, written work, participation, notebooks or workbooks, and DeltaMath.

Tests may be retaken for a higher grade. Retakes are intended to motivate students to keep learning after mistakes and to demonstrate stronger understanding after additional practice.

07 · Getting help

It is okay not to understand—yet

Students should let Mr. Chen know when they are stuck. Asking for help is an important part of becoming an independent learner.

Challenge is expected. Silence is not required.

Mr. Chen will look for another pathway when a student needs more support. This may include a smaller set of problems, another model or explanation, guided practice, additional time, or support from a classmate.

08 · Phones and electronics

Protecting learning time

The school-day device routine supports focus, safety, and a respectful learning environment.

Cell phones are turned in during homeroom.

Students must turn in their cell phones to their homeroom teacher at the beginning of the school day. Phones are placed in secured, locked storage according to the school's procedure.

A special circumstance requiring device access must be communicated in advance so the parent or guardian, teachers, and appropriate school staff are aware. Other personal electronic devices may not be used unless the school has specifically approved the device as an instructional or accessibility tool.
