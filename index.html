02 · Course overview

What we will learn

Our year moves from visual and geometric reasoning into proportional reasoning, number operations, algebra, coordinate geometry, and statistics. Select any unit to see its major topics.

1 Area & Surface Area +
  • Decomposing and rearranging shapes
  • Area of parallelograms, triangles, and polygons
  • Polyhedra, nets, surface area, and volume
  • Squares, cubes, and the designing-a-tent application
2 Ratios +
  • Ratio language and diagrams
  • Equivalent ratios
  • Double number lines and tables
  • Unit rates, constant speed, and ratio problems
3 Rates & Percentages +
  • Units of measurement and unit conversion
  • Comparing speeds and prices
  • Interpreting and solving rate problems
  • Percentages, tape diagrams, and percent problems
4 Fraction Division +
  • Making sense of division situations
  • Meaning and diagrams of fraction division
  • Fraction-division algorithms
  • Fractional lengths, area, and volume
5 Decimal Operations +
  • Decimals in real-world contexts
  • Adding and subtracting decimals
  • Multiplying decimals with diagrams and algorithms
  • Partial quotients, long division, and decimal division
6 Expressions & Equations +
  • Tape diagrams and one-variable equations
  • Equality, equivalence, and balance
  • The distributive property
  • Exponents, expressions, tables, equations, and graphs
7 Rational Numbers +
  • Positive and negative numbers
  • Absolute value and inequalities
  • The coordinate plane and distance
  • Common factors and common multiples
8 Data & Statistics +
  • Statistical questions and variability
  • Dot plots and histograms
  • Mean, median, MAD, and IQR
  • Quartiles, box plots, and comparing distributions
9 Applications & Connections +
  • Fermi problems and estimation
  • “If Our Class Were the World”
  • Rectangle investigations
  • Voting methods and choosing representatives
03 · Learning habits

What I expect from students

Students do not need to know everything immediately. They do need to arrive prepared, participate, make an honest attempt, and help create a classroom where everyone can think and learn.

During math class

  • Arrive on time with all daily materials ready.
  • Attempt each problem and show mathematical thinking.
  • Explain ideas, listen to classmates, and ask questions.
  • Use feedback to revise work and strengthen understanding.
  • Treat classmates, adults, materials, and learning time with respect.

How families can help

  • Check Google Classroom regularly for announcements and assignments.
  • Ask your student to explain a problem or strategy from their notebook.
  • Help your student maintain the daily notebook-and-pencil routine.
  • Encourage consistent practice, even when a problem feels challenging.
  • Contact me early when questions, circumstances, or concerns arise.

05 · School-day focus

Cell phones and electronics

This procedure protects students' attention and supports a focused, respectful learning environment throughout the school day.

Important family information

Cell phones are turned in and secured at the beginning of the school day.

Students must turn in their cell phones to their homeroom teacher during homeroom. Phones will be placed in secured, locked storage according to the school's procedure.

If a student has a special circumstance requiring access to a device, it must be communicated in advance so that the parent or guardian, teachers, and appropriate school staff are aware.

Other personal electronic devices may not be used unless the school has specifically approved the device as an instructional or accessibility tool. Families who need to reach a student during the school day should use the school's approved communication process.
